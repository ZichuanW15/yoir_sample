# Risk Register

This document records the initial risk assessment for the Yoir Career Advisory Platform, carried out after the first client meeting on 25-08-2026. It covers the two areas with the greatest technical uncertainty at this stage — counsellor verification through LinkedIn, and payment processing — now that the client has confirmed the product will be a native iOS and Android application.

Each risk is rated for likelihood and impact and paired with the mitigation the team is carrying into Sprint 2. This is a first pass: risks will be reviewed and re-rated at each sprint planning, and new risks added as the design and executable architecture mature.

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | LinkedIn's public API does not expose what verification needs — Sign In with LinkedIn returns only name, photo and email; richer profile data requires Partner Program approval, which is slow and rarely granted to student projects. | High | High | Baseline on LinkedIn URL submission + manual Platform Owner review. Treat API integration as a spike, not a dependency. |
| R-02 | Submitted LinkedIn URLs can point to someone else's profile or a fabricated one, and manual review creates a bottleneck at counsellor onboarding. | Medium | High | Require the URL to match the registered name; reviewer opens the profile directly; allow rejection with reason and resubmission. Let counsellors draft profile and services while pending. |
| R-03 | A live payment account (Stripe or equivalent) needs a registered business, bank account and identity checks the team cannot provide, and paying counsellors requires a separate marketplace product (e.g. Stripe Connect) with per-counsellor KYC. | High | High | Build against the provider's test mode behind a payment boundary; live keys are a config swap. Scope the MVP to platform-supervised purchase only — payouts are recorded, not executed. Confirm the payment model and account ownership with the client. |
| R-04 | App store rules — Apple and Google require in-app purchase (15–30% commission) for services consumed inside the app. Counselling sessions are real-world services and normally exempt, but reviewers can disagree. | Medium | High | Position services as booked sessions delivered outside the app (online call or in person); use the provider's mobile SDK, not IAP; cite the guideline relied on in submission notes. |
| R-05 | Payment succeeds but the booking is not confirmed (or the reverse) if a webhook is missed or a request is retried — a paid customer with no slot. | Medium | High | Order and booking state machine with idempotent webhook handling; a failed or unknown payment never yields a confirmed booking. Covered by the failure-injection and concurrency tests. |

## Sprint 3 review (18-10-2026)

Reviewed at the end of Sprint 3 against the code on `dev` and the Week 11 client meeting (16-10-2026). The table above is kept as the Sprint 1 baseline; the ratings below are the current ones.

### Status of the original risks

| ID | Status | Likelihood | Impact | Sprint 3 notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | Mitigated | Low | Medium | Verification is a LinkedIn URL submission plus manual Platform Owner review (US-31, US-32, US-33, US-58), with no dependency on the LinkedIn API. The client agreed this approach (Requirements RR-05) and saw the approve and reject flow demoed on 16-10-2026. |
| R-02 | Partly mitigated | Medium | High | In place: server-side LinkedIn URL validation, rejection with a required reason, resubmission returns the submission to pending, and profile and availability can be edited while pending. Still open: nothing checks that the URL matches the registered name (the reviewer judges it); a verified counsellor can change their LinkedIn URL without going back to pending; rejecting or re-opening a verification does not hide services already listed; services can only be created once verified, so there is no draft state. |
| R-03 | Mitigated for the MVP | Medium | High | Stripe runs in test mode behind a single gateway module, and the publishable key is served by the backend, so going live is a key swap. Scope changed from Sprint 1: counsellor payouts are now executed as Stripe Connect test-mode transfers 24 hours after the session, not only recorded. Still open with the client: commission settings and `on_behalf_of` (waiting on the client's accountant), the late-cancellation refund policy, and who owns the live Stripe account. |
| R-04 | Deferred | Low (this semester) | High | No App Store or Google Play release this semester; the app is deployed as an Expo web build served by FastAPI. No in-app purchase is used, and the payment spec relies on Apple guideline 3.1.3(d). There is no EAS build configuration yet. Re-assess before any store release. |
| R-05 | Mitigated | Low | High | Stripe idempotency keys per order, transfer and refund; webhook signatures are verified and each event is applied once; slot reservation takes a row lock with a 15-minute hold; a payment that lands after the slot was taken is refunded. Covered by `backend/tests/test_payments.py`, `test_api.py`, `test_reschedule.py` and the Sprint 3 recovery QA tests. Residual: no database-level constraint against overlapping holds (recorded as an expected-failure test), and the concurrency tests only run on Postgres. |

### New risks identified in Sprint 3

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-06 | Diagnosis rules are not confirmed by the client. The six-step questions and direction rules (US-10 to US-13) are hard-coded from the team's reading of the requirements (`yoir-six-step-v1`, `yoir-direction-v1`). Requirements RR-13 and RR-14 are still open, the client's topic-bank workbook is not imported, and on 16-10-2026 the client said he is rewriting the questions himself. Late changes before acceptance on 23-10-2026 are likely. | High | High | The rules are versioned and kept in one module (`backend/app/diagnosis.py`), so a new question set or rule table is a contained change. Get the client's revised questions in writing, confirm the rules before acceptance, and list anything not changed in time as a known limitation in the handover. |
| R-07 | Insecure default configuration. Out of the box the backend falls back to a development JWT secret with no start-up check, allows CORS from any origin, leaves the API docs page on, and seeds demo accounts with a shared password on start-up. Demo account credentials are also written in the repository docs, and the demo deployment is protected only by its URL not being shared. | Medium | High | Refuse to start outside local development when the JWT secret is the default; restrict CORS to the deployed origin; turn off seeding and the docs page in deployed environments; remove credentials from the repository docs and rotate the admin password; include a configuration checklist in the handover. |
| R-08 | Dependency vulnerabilities. CI only fails on critical npm advisories in production dependencies, there is no Python dependency audit and no Dependabot, so a vulnerable package could ship unnoticed, and the handed-over code will age once the team stops maintaining it. | Medium | Medium | Lockfiles (`uv.lock`, `package-lock.json`) and SHA-pinned GitHub Actions already keep builds reproducible. Add Dependabot for npm, pip and GitHub Actions, add `pip-audit` to CI, and run a full audit before handover with the results recorded. |
| R-09 | Sandbox and free-tier limits. The Mailgun sandbox only delivers to authorised recipients; Stripe is in test mode (test cards only, and Connect onboarding is needed before a counsellor can be booked); Neon's serverless Postgres drops idle connections after about 5 minutes and the app's connection pool does not yet guard against it; FastAPI Cloud free-tier limits are unconfirmed; there is no rate limiting. The client could hit failed emails, errors after idle periods or capacity limits during demos and acceptance testing. | High | Medium | Add the client's test addresses as authorised Mailgun recipients before demos; enable `pool_pre_ping` on the database engine; warm up the deployment before demos; document each sandbox limit and the steps to move to production accounts in the handover. |
| R-10 | Handover of accounts and secrets. The code will be copied into a new repository the client creates and also delivered as a zip (Week 11 decision), but the FastAPI Cloud, Neon, Stripe and Mailgun accounts and their secrets sit with the team. | Medium | High | List every external account, who owns it and which settings and secrets it needs in the handover document; never put secret values in the repository or the zip; agree with the client which accounts are transferred and which are recreated under the client's ownership. |
